¿What's the anatomy of a good tool? This questions was haunting me while building internal automation tools for our company.

A bad one stands in the way. It stops, barricades, asks for attention. It halts momentum and throw your flow. If we ever arrive at destination, we do it while exhausted. If a car gives too many issues, it's no better than a bike.

A good tool however is a shortcut to a destination. It becomes invisible in its motion. It disappears while giving mental space. We do not think about the motion of the steering wheel while driving a car, as a consequence the brain has space left to think about the road ahead.

A pianist doesn't think about individual notes. Instead the chords and scales are ingrained in its motor skills. As a consequence, the brain has space to think about the melody.

Ever notice how you forget about your chair? An object so well designed it's the shape of the task itself, rest. A good rule of thumb, if your team doesn't notice your tool's absence, keep iterating until they do.

I once fought my computer, it was like dragging a rusty machete throught thick vines. I never sharpened the blade. Until I realised: keyboard shortcuts aren’t tricks. They’re _hidden currents_, carrying part of my burden up to completion.”

Most software still _interrupts_. Its a constant attention seeking machine with buttons, menus, menus, clutter. Yet with AI we seem closer to close the gap between intention and result. A simple chat box does the magic almost closing the gap between intention and destination.

Good Tools I cannot work without: keybindings.json on vscode (uhum cursor), Vim motions, Alfred.
